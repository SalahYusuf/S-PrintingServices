About SPS

Printing built around student life.

Salah Printing Service (SPS) is a student-focused printing business serving the MMU Cyberjaya community with straightforward A4 printing, lamination and convenient collection or delivery. Our role is intentionally focused: customers provide their documents; SPS handles the physical print production and fulfilment.

The brand combines a traditional print service with a digital-first ordering experience. Our website reduces friction at the point of purchase, while social media is used to make pricing, service instructions and time-sensitive updates easy to discover.

1 · Business Overview

What SPS actually is.

SPS is not a graphic-design agency. It is a print-production service that competes on convenience, price clarity and an ordering experience tailored to students.

Our business model

Customers submit completed documents or print-ready files. SPS prints the requested quantity, optionally laminates the output, then fulfils the order through self-pickup or delivery around MMU Cyberjaya.

1
A4 PrintingBlack & white or colour, single- or double-sided.
2
LaminationOptional protection for selected printed sheets.
3
FulfilmentSelf-pickup or door-to-door delivery around campus.

Why “Marketing Business”?

The assignment provides only three broad categories. SPS is positioned under Marketing Business because the business is built around identifying a specific market, communicating a clear value proposition, reaching that market through digital channels and converting attention into orders.

Important positioning

“Marketing Business” is the assignment classification. The operational service itself remains printing only. SPS does not claim to provide campaign strategy or graphic-design services.

Research Context

Why a campus-first model is reasonable.

Our strategy is based on a narrow local market rather than trying to compete as a generic commercial printer across Malaysia.

12,000+
MMU students across Cyberjaya and Melaka

MMU’s publication portal describes the university as having more than 12,000 students across its two campuses. This is not a Cyberjaya-only figure, so SPS treats it as broad institutional context rather than its addressable market size.

100+
Clubs and societies in MMU’s wider campus ecosystem

MMU materials describe a large club-and-society ecosystem. This matters because student organisations create recurring demand for printed notices, event materials, schedules and administrative documents.

30.7m
Social media user identities in Malaysia

Malaysia has very high social-media adoption. For a campus business, that supports a digital-first discovery model instead of relying only on physical posters or word of mouth.

Research basis: MMU Publication, MMU International Student Guide, DataReportal Digital 2026: Malaysia.

4 · Target Audience

We do not target “everyone”.

SPS focuses first on MMU Cyberjaya students. Within that market, we design the service around three practical use cases rather than one generic student profile.

A

Assignment-Driven Student

Needs lecture notes, reports, forms or assignment materials printed close to a deadline. The key value is speed, price clarity and minimal ordering effort.

Primary need: fast, predictable printing without unnecessary travel.
C

Club / Committee Member

Handles meeting documents, schedules, certificates, checklists or event materials. Orders may involve more pages or repeated sets.

Primary need: reliable repeat orders, quantity handling and convenient collection.
H

Hostel / Convenience Buyer

May have no personal printer or may prefer not to travel solely to print a small number of documents.

Primary need: door-to-door convenience for low-friction everyday printing.
Research vs. assumption

The existence of student printing demand is supported by Malaysian university printing services and prior campus-printing business projects. The three personas above are SPS working hypotheses and should be validated through a short survey or order data if the business were launched for real.

2, 3 & 6 · Digital Presence Strategy

Each channel has one job.

The website and social platform should not duplicate one another. SPS uses the website as the conversion utility and Instagram as the discovery and trust layer.

Customer journey

DiscoverStudent sees an SPS post, Story, Reel or shared recommendation.
2

3
EvaluateThe website provides full information and an instant cost estimate.
4
ConvertCustomer submits or saves an order request.
5
ReturnUseful content, updates and easy re-ordering encourage repeat use.
Recommended Main Platform

Instagram — awareness + trust

Instagram suits SPS because the assignment needs a mix of posts, Reel and Story content, while the service itself benefits from visual price cards, step-by-step carousels, time-sensitive Stories and short process videos. DataReportal reports 16.1 million Instagram users in Malaysia in late 2025, with ad reach equivalent to 58.4% of adults aged 18+.

Website

Website — conversion

The website answers the questions that block a purchase: what it costs, what SPS prints, how to order, where to collect and how delivery works. The quote calculator turns the website into a utility rather than a static brochure.

Why not only social?

Owned information

A website gives SPS one stable source of truth for pricing and order instructions. Social posts can then point customers back to the same information instead of repeating full details in every caption.

Platform context: Digital 2026: Malaysia . Instagram figures are advertising-reach estimates, not monthly active-user counts.

5 & 6 · Content Strategy

Content should remove reasons not to order.

Instead of posting only advertisements, SPS content is divided into four jobs. This creates a more useful social account and gives the team enough variation for the required posts, Reel and Story.

01

Price Clarity

Make the cost easy to understand before a customer sends a message.

Example: “A4 B&W from RM0.30 — single vs double-sided explained.”
02

How-To Content

Reduce ordering mistakes by showing the process, file requirements and collection choices.

Example carousel: “How to order from SPS in 5 steps.”
03

Convenience & Proof

Show the actual service outcome, collection process or packaging to make SPS feel real and dependable.

Example Reel: file received → printed pages → packed order → pickup.
04

Time-Sensitive Updates

Use Stories for operating status, same-day capacity, delivery slots or temporary notices.

Example Story: “Orders before 2 PM: collect this evening.”

7 · SMART Goals & KPIs

What success would look like.

These are proposed targets for the assignment, not claimed historical results. They are designed around a simple funnel: awareness → engagement → website intent → enquiry / order.

SMART Goal Why It Matters Primary KPIs Target Timeframe
Build local awareness
Reach MMU-relevant audiences through SPS Instagram content.		 A new service must first become discoverable before it can generate enquiries. Accounts reached, impressions, follower growth Reach at least 1,500 accounts and gain 100 followers. Within 6 weeks
Create useful engagement
Publish content students save or share, not only like.		 Shares and saves indicate practical value for price lists and ordering guides. Saves, shares, comments, engagement rate Generate at least 60 combined saves + shares across assessed content. Within 6 weeks
Drive website intent
Turn social discovery into visits to the quote page.		 The website is the conversion layer, so traffic from Instagram shows the channels are connected. Profile link clicks, website visits, quote-page visits Generate at least 50 website visits from social content. Within 6 weeks
Generate enquiries / quote actions
Convert a portion of interested visitors into measurable intent.		 Orders are the end goal; quote requests and enquiries are the closest measurable pre-purchase actions. Quote requests, DMs, contact submissions Generate at least 15 combined quote requests or direct enquiries. Within 6 weeks
Why these KPIs?

Followers alone are not enough. SPS tracks the full path from reach to action. Saves and shares test whether content is useful; website visits test whether social content creates intent; enquiries and quote requests test whether that intent is commercial.
